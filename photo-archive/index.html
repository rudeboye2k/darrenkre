How each match was proven

A filename that reads 92 Ralph Ave.jpg is a claim, not proof. Every original was tested against the image actually published on the website using a perceptual comparison — both images reduced to a 16×16 grayscale fingerprint and correlated pixel by pixel.

A score of 1.000 means the two files are the same photograph. Anything below roughly 0.90 would mean the filename was lying. Every one of the ten scored 0.999 or higher.

Yes. The archive was swept exhaustively, and the leftovers were checked rather than assumed.

Of the 967 genuinely distinct images, most are not photographs at all — 341 are navigation thumbnails, 132 are website furniture (buttons, arrows, spacers), and 297 are too small to be useful. That leaves 196 real photographs. 124 of those are now filed under an address. The remaining 73 were each checked three ways before being set aside:

  1. Perceptual comparison. All 73 were correlated against all ten reference images. The highest score anywhere was 0.572 — far below the 0.99 that indicates a match. None is a copy of a portfolio photo.
  2. Timestamp cross-check. None falls inside the March 2009 shoot window that produced the ten exteriors.
  3. Visual review. All 73 were laid out on a single contact sheet and examined by eye, because a photograph of the same building from a different angle would score low and slip past the first two tests. They turned out to be other properties (1115 Herkimer, 296 Alabama, Cambria Heights, a waterfront aerial, a granite kitchen) and roughly 30 scanned pages from an unrelated family reunion calendar.

Nothing depicting the ten addresses was left behind.

Interiors, and an important limitation

One property gained interior photography. The other nine cannot, and the reason matters.

1058 Putnam Avenue — 10 interior shots

Kitchen, bathroom, entrance hall, front room, hallways and hutch, all at full resolution, roughly 3 MB each. The gallery metadata titles this set "Darren K Real Estate 3Br for Rent" and names the street but never a house number. Since 1058 Putnam is the only Putnam property in the portfolio, these were filed there.

Why the other nine have none

The remaining interior galleries belong to the portfolio as a whole, not to any single building. Three independent pieces of evidence say so:

  1. The captions describe unit types, not addresses. They read Howard Ave. Cluster Studio Apt., … 1 Bedroom Apt., … 2 Bedroom Apt.
  2. The old website was organised that way by design. Its navigation ran Howard Avenue Cluster → 11 CLUSTER UNITS → STUDIO / ONE BEDROOM / TWO BEDROOM. Filed by layout, never by building.
  3. "Howard Ave" is the operator, not the location. The archived page Howard Ave Properties/index.html states: "These properties are managed by Howard Avenue Development LP", and its thumbnail folder contains all ten portfolio buildings. The "LP" in those folder names is that limited partnership.

What this rules out "Howard Ave. Cluster" does not mean 50 Howard Avenue. Filing those interiors under 50 Howard would have been wrong. EXIF was checked as a fallback — the gallery files are stripped to 640×425 with no capture date and no caption data, and their filenames are content hashes such as _0405445962.jpg. There is nothing left to attribute them with, so they were not attributed.
