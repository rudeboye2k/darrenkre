The short version

The good news. The pictures on your website are small, shrunken copies. The proper full-size versions were still sitting in the old website files — and they are far better. We have recovered one for every single building and filed them properly.

The bad news. That is very nearly all we have. Almost everything else in the old website is a shrunken copy too — small pictures that look fine on a screen but would come out blurry in a brochure. Altogether we found 428 pictures. 369 of them print no bigger than a postcard.

What that means in practice. What you actually own is roughly one photograph of each building, all taken on the same afternoon in March 2009. No photographs inside nine of the ten buildings. Nothing showing any work done since. And 92 Ralph — your own office — has the worst picture of the lot.

The one thing to do. Call Erl. Details are further down, but the short of it is that the originals from his camera were never kept on the website. If he still has them, they would be far better than anything we found.

The phone call worth making

The old website names the photographer in three separate places: E R Lessey.

He is credited on the photo gallery page from August 2008, named inside four gallery files from 2010 and 2011, and folder names from his own computer are still buried in the website's pages — so he built the site as well as taking the pictures.

Here is why that matters. Everything we found is a copy that was made for the website — already shrunk down to load quickly. The originals straight out of his camera were never stored on the website at all. They only ever existed on his own computer.

If he still has them — on a hard drive, an old computer, or camera cards in a drawer — they would be far better than anything we have. They would also likely include photographs he took but never put online: rooms, other angles, buildings that never made it onto the site.

What to ask him for

  • The original photos straight from the camera — not the ones from the website.
  • Anything from the day in March 2009 when he photographed all the buildings in one go.
  • The apartment interiors — the studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom sets. We have them, but no way to tell which building each is in. He may simply remember.

It has been sixteen years, so he may well not have them any more. But it is one phone call, and a yes would save paying a photographer to start from scratch.

If the answer is no Then it is worth booking a photographer — starting with 92 Ralph, since your own office currently has the weakest picture of the ten. A single morning would cover the lot.

Corroboration from the camera data

Eight of the ten exteriors carry timestamps inside a single 54-second window on 25 March 2009 (13:54:28 – 13:55:22) — one pass down the street. 1058 Putnam followed five days later. 92 Ralph was shot separately in September 2009 and only survives at 1000×1400, already downsized.

Completeness

1,435 image files were scanned; 967 are unique by content. Of those, 341 are navigation thumbnails, 132 are website furniture, and the rest are photographs. The 428 shown here are every photograph above 8 KB after filtering out interface graphics.

Photographs not filed under an address were each checked three ways: perceptual comparison against all ten references (highest score anywhere 0.572, far below the 0.99 match threshold), capture-timestamp cross-check against the March 2009 window, and visual review of a contact sheet — because a different-angle shot of the same building would score low and pass the first two tests.

Why the interiors cannot be assigned

The apartment galleries are captioned by unit type (Howard Ave. Cluster Studio Apt.), the old site's navigation organised them the same way (11 CLUSTER UNITS → STUDIO / ONE BEDROOM / TWO BEDROOM), and "Howard Ave" refers to the operator, not the location — the page Howard Ave Properties/index.html states the properties are managed by Howard Avenue Development LP, and its thumbnail folder contains all ten buildings. The gallery files are stripped to 640×425 with no EXIF and content-hash filenames, so there is nothing left to attribute them with.
