The problem, in one sentence

Of the 428 pictures we have, 369 print no bigger than a postcard.

428Pictures we have
50Big enough for a poster
369Postcard size or smaller
16Years since they were taken

When a photo is put on a website it gets shrunk down, because a big file would make the page slow to load. That is what happened here, years ago — and the shrinking throws away detail permanently. You cannot get it back later. Blowing one of these up for a brochure would look blurry and pixelated.

Scroll down and you will see the pattern for yourself: page after page of small, soft pictures. What we own at a decent size comes to roughly one photo of each building, all taken on the same afternoon in March 2009.

What we simply do not have

  • No photos inside nine of the ten buildings. Only 1058 Putnam has rooms we can match to an address.
  • One angle of each building. Every photo is the front of the building taken from across the street. Nothing else — no rear, no detail, no interiors.
  • 92 Ralph is the worst of the lot. Your own office. The best copy we have prints to about 3″ × 5″ — smaller than a postcard.
  • Nothing since 2009. Any work done to the buildings in sixteen years does not appear anywhere.
  • One file has gone bad. A photo of 652 Decatur St is still listed by name, but the picture inside it is gone — the file is empty. It is shown below with a cross-hatched box.

Who took these, and why it's worth calling him

The old website names its photographer in three separate places: E R Lessey. He is credited on the photo gallery page from August 2008, named in four gallery files from 2010 and 2011, and his own computer's folder names are still buried in the website's pages — so he built the site as well.

The important part Every picture on this page is a copy made for the website — already shrunk. The originals straight out of his camera were never kept on the website. They only ever existed on his own computer.

If he still has them — on a hard drive, an old computer, or the camera cards in a drawer — they would be far better than anything here, and would probably include shots he took but never put online. Rooms, other angles, buildings that never made the site.

What to ask him for: the original photos straight from the camera (not the ones off the website), anything from the day in March 2009 when he photographed all the buildings, and the apartment interiors — the studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom sets. Those are on this page but we cannot tell which building they belong to. He may just remember.

It has been sixteen years, so he may well not have them. But it costs one phone call, and a yes would save paying a photographer to start again.
