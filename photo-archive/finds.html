The case for reshooting

The archive is not empty. It is thin in exactly the places that matter, and these four numbers are the whole argument.

428Image files found
50Good enough to print
368Web resolution only
16Years since the shoot

Six out of every seven files in this archive are unusable for anything but a web page. They top out below 1500 pixels on the longest edge. That detail was thrown away years ago when the images were resized for the website, and no amount of software will bring it back. Scroll down and the pattern is obvious — page after page of small, soft, sixteen-year-old exports.

What survives at full size comes to roughly one photograph per building, taken on a single afternoon in March 2009.

What is actually missing

  • No interiors for nine of the ten buildings. Only 1058 Putnam has rooms that can be tied to an address.
  • One angle per building. Every portfolio photograph is a single street-level shot of the façade — no detail, no rear, no block context, no seasons.
  • 92 Ralph has no full-size original at all. The head office survives only at 1000×1400, already downsized before it was archived.
  • Sixteen years of change is unrecorded. Any renovation, repointing or streetscape change since March 2009 is invisible.
  • One file has rotted. 652 DECATUR ST.jpg is 20 KB of which 99.9% is zero bytes — the image is gone, only the filename survives. A second copy elsewhere in the archive is intact, which is luck rather than backup policy.

Who took these — and what to ask him for

The archive names its photographer, and the same person appears to have built the original website.

Why this is worth a phone call Everything on this page is a web export — resized, recompressed, and in the gallery sets stripped of camera data entirely. The camera originals were never stored here. If E R Lessey still holds the source files, the memory cards, or an old drive, those files would be far better than anything shown here, and they would almost certainly include frames that were shot but never published — interiors, other angles, buildings that never made it onto the site.

It has been sixteen years and the odds are honestly uncertain. But it costs one phone call, and a yes would save reshooting the entire portfolio.

Worth asking for specifically: the original camera files (not the website copies), anything shot on the March 2009 portfolio day, and any interior sets — the studio, one-bedroom and two-bedroom galleries, which he may remember by building.
